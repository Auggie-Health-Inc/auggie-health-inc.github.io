Editorial & Peer-Review Policy

Augmented Intelligence operates transparent, open peer review. Our aim is that a reader can see exactly how an article was vetted.

Review model

  • Each article is reviewed by at least one qualified external reviewer, plus a structured AI adversarial-review pass that stress-tests claims, figures and sources.
  • An editor who is not an author signs every decision.
  • Review notes and reviewer dispositions are published alongside the article.

Conflict of interest — the founder rule

Articles authored or co-authored by the journal's publisher or editor-in-chief are handled by an independent editor and external reviewers only. The authoring party has no role in the editorial decision, and that handling is stated on the article. This exists to counter the natural discount a reader applies to a journal publishing its founder's work.

AI-assistance disclosure

Every article carries a mandatory statement describing what AI tools did and who is accountable for the content. Transparency about method is a condition of publication, not an optional footnote.

How review actually works — and our honesty about AI

We do not hide that this is an AI-assisted operation; the transparency is the point.

  • A human is always in the loop. Nothing publishes without human sign-off.
  • A reviewer's job is the high-level critiques only an expert can make — the two or three points that actually change the work. The mechanical rest (formatting, consistency checks, first-pass tightening) is handled by AI.
  • This is the workflow we run in practice: an expert reviewer can spend a few minutes, add a handful of guiding points, and get a draft most of the way there, then do a final pass in their own voice. We institutionalize that, and we disclose it on every article.

This is not a lower bar. It concentrates human judgment where it is scarce and valuable, and it is stated openly so readers can weigh it.

Reviewers get credit — open review as a citable credential

The hardest thing to get in research is sharp, expert critique. The way we earn it is to make reviewing worth the reviewer's time by making it visible and creditable, not anonymous unpaid labor.

  • Opt-in open review. A reviewer may choose to have their named critique published alongside the article, registered with its own Crossref DOI (Crossref supports peer-review DOIs, and they are inexpensive). The review becomes a citable output on the reviewer's own record, not invisible work.
  • Reviewer of record. Named recognition on the article and a contributor profile on our board / network page, so a reviewer's judgment becomes part of their public scholarly brand.
  • A reputation surface. Over time, a reviewer's published critiques form a visible body of work that builds their standing.

We are honest about the mechanism: a strong, public critique advances the reviewer's own brand as much as the author's, and that alignment is deliberate — it is the engine that produces the high-quality peer feedback the whole system runs on. Reviewers who prefer to remain anonymous may; the credit path is opt-in.

Corrections

Post-publication corrections are issued transparently and versioned; the DOI always resolves to the current version with a visible change note.
